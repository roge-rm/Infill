# Districts
> Painting districts, their taxes, height limits and policies.

From the Streetcar era you can paint the town into districts and give each its own rules. Under **Zones**, **Districts**, pick **New district** and paint, or pick a district to paint more of it, or **Erase**. Painting is free. A district painted down to nothing goes away.

**Districts** in the tray lists them. Each card shows its people and jobs, its land value, crime and pollution, and its settings.

## Taxes

Each district can set its homes, shops and offices, and works taxes up to five points either side of the town's. Each point lower makes the district a bit more appealing, and each point higher a bit less.

## Height limit

**Height limit** keeps a district no denser than low, medium or high density, whatever the zoning says. Taller buildings already there stop fitting and come down in time.

## Policies

- **Keep heritage**: heritage buildings stay as they are. From 1970, the solid brick and stone buildings put up before 1930 count as heritage. Clearing one upsets the whole district.
- **Limit parking**: fewer people drive to and from the district.
- **No heavy industry**: works grow no bigger than a mill.
- **Pollution limit on works**: works put out much less pollution and carbon, but they like the district less.
- **No heavy trucks**: trucks stay off its streets unless they have business there.
- **Free fares**: rides on trams, buses and trolleybuses from the district are free, so more people take them.
- **Rent control**: homes go only to poor and middling households, and pay less tax.
- **Cool roofs and paving** (from 1990): the district stays cooler in summer, and homes use less power.
- **Green roofs** (from 2000): cooler still, they hold back some of the rain, homes like them, and they use less power.

The roof policies cost a little upkeep for each building.
