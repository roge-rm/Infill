# Transit and rail
> Trams, buses, trolleybuses, the subway, and trains.

People go the quickest way they can. Those with a car can drive, and everyone walks or rides. Transit is how a town gets people to work before the car, and how it keeps the roads moving after.

## How people choose

There's no choosing as such. Each trip takes whatever gets there soonest, counting the walk to the stop, the wait and the ride. Walking is slow, a minute a tile. A trip is counted as the best way it used, so someone who walks to the tram is a tram rider.

## Trams

Under **Transport**, **Transit** and the **Trams** tab:

1. Lay **Tram track** along roads.
2. Put a **Tram depot** beside the track. It needs power and holds 12 trams.
3. Put **Tram stop** on the track where people should get on.
4. Pick **Tram line**, tap the stops in order, and **Make the line**. The trams run from the first stop to the last and back.

Trams are slowed by the traffic on their street, unless the street has a **Bus and tram lane**, which leaves 60% of the road for cars.

## Buses and trolleybuses

Buses come with the Streetcar era, from 1920. Put a **Bus garage** (it holds 20 buses) and **Bus stop** along roads, then make a **Bus line** the same way as a tram line. Buses use the roads and sit in traffic like everyone else, and stopping slows them a little.

From 1925, **Trolleybus wire** over a bus line's whole route, joined to a powered garage, runs it as a trolleybus line: no fumes in the street, though it draws power.

## The subway

From the Streetcar era, the **Subway** tab lays **Subway** tunnel under anything but water, and **Subway station** on top. A station needs power and a road. The subway is fast, ignores traffic, and runs in a blizzard.

## Lines and vehicles

A line runs only if its first stop is joined to a working depot or garage. A new line suggests how many vehicles it needs for its length. More vehicles mean shorter waits, and crowded ones longer waits. Each tram costs $4,000 and each bus $1,500, plus a little a month, and taking vehicles off sells them back at half price. **Lines** in the tray opens the list of lines, with their riders, round trip and wait.

Every ride on a tram, bus, trolleybus or subway pays a fare of 30 cents, under **Fares** in the budget. The **Free fares** district policy lets people ride free in that district, and the ride feels shorter to them, so more take it.

Stops raise land value nearby, and subway stations raise it more. From 2000, mixed use near a stop does better.

## Railways

Under **Transport**, **Rail**:

- **Track** is dragged like a road. It crosses a road only straight over it, at a level crossing, which holds up the traffic.
- **Station** goes along a stretch of track, on its long side. Trains run between stations on the same line.
- **Freight yard** sends the works' freight out of town by rail.
- **Freight terminal** (from 1965) does more, and helps works within 10 tiles.

A line with track on the edge of the map runs trains to and from outside. A station on such a line brings newcomers, visitors and more homes wanted, and a freight yard or terminal on one grows the market for the town's works. Rail rides don't pay fares.

Trains run on steam until 1955, then diesel.

Stations, yards and ports need a phone from 1920, power from 1930 and broadband from 2005 to work their best, and stop altogether below half.
