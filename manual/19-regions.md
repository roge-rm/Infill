# Regions
> Towns side by side, and what crosses between them.

A region is a set of towns side by side on one piece of land, two by two, three by three or four by four. Lakes, rivers, the coast and woods carry across the borders. You play one town at a time, and the others stay as you left them.

## Making a region

**New city**, then **A region of towns**. Pick a name, the grid, how big each town is, and the land. **Start** shows the region's map.

Tap a square to pick it. An empty one has **Found a town here**, and a town has **Play**. **Region** in the game's menu saves the town and goes back to the map.

## Borders

Past each edge with a town beyond it, a strip of the neighbour shows: its land, roads, track, lines and mains. A green dot on the border marks a link, a place where your road or track meets theirs on the same tile. A road kept in town makes no link. Only links carry anything, so line your roads, track, lines and mains up with your neighbours'.

## What crosses

Each month, and when a town is loaded, it works out what goes across:

- **Commuters**: people out of work take jobs next door, and next door's take yours.
- **Shoppers**: a town short of shops shops next door, and one with shops to spare draws them in.
- **Goods**: what a neighbour has spare comes in before anything from outside, and what a neighbour's short of goes to them.
- **Power and water**: sold to a neighbour that's short over lines and mains that meet, and bought when you're short.
- **Garbage**: sent to a neighbour's dumps over the roads, for a fee, when yours can't take it.
- **Smog, pollution, noise and foul water** drift over from a neighbour.

A ledger keeps each neighbour's share straight, so two towns can't take the same jobs or the same spare power. The People window and the budget show what's crossing: who's working and shopping next door, goods, and power, water and garbage bought and sold.
