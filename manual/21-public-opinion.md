# Public opinion
> Approval, what people mind, petitions, grants, protests and elections.

## Approval

Approval is what the town thinks of how it's run, from 0 to 100. It's under **People**, and **Opinion** there shows what makes it up. It moves a little each month towards what people think now, so a change takes a while to show.

People think about ten things: taxes, jobs, crime, health, power and water, things to do, traffic, clearances, the air and housing. Housing counts those looking for a home and those sleeping rough. What counts most changes with the era:

- **Township**: health, taxes and the services.
- **Streetcar city**: much the same, with a little traffic and the air.
- **Motor age**: jobs and traffic.
- **Renewal**: the clearances, jobs and taxes.
- **Infill** and after: housing, the air and things to do.

Below 40, the advice line says what people are unhappiest about.

The **Mood** view shows how content each home is, red to green. A district's card in the Districts window shows its mood and what its people mind most.

## Petitions

Now and then, once the town has 500 people, a neighbourhood short of something asks for it: a park, police, a fire station, mains water, the sewer, power, or a tram or bus stop. It's in the news, and in **Opinion** with the month it's wanted by. Tap it to go there. Give them what they asked for within the year and approval goes up; let it lapse and it goes down. There can be two at once.

## Grants

When approval is 55 or more, higher government may offer a grant toward the work of the day, once each:

- **Sewers**, 1930 to 1950: paid once enough more of the town is on the sewer.
- **Highways**, 1950 to 1975: once 30 more tiles of highway are built.
- **Transit**, 1970 to 2000: once more trips are made other than by car.
- **Storm drains**, from 2010: once 60 more tiles of storm drain are laid.

A grant is six months of the town's income, and the goal has to be met within three years. **Opinion** shows the one on offer and how far along the town is. A grant paid raises approval a little, and one let lapse lowers it.

## Protests

From the Renewal era on, clearing a heritage building or six or more homes' lots at once brings people out to protest, and approval drops. If approval is already under 40, the protesters stop the works and it can't go ahead.

## Elections

Turn on **Hold elections** in **Opinion** for an election every four years, in November. Each grant paid since the last election adds 5 to the vote. At 45 or more the council is returned, and higher government offers it the next grant of the era straight away. Below that it's voted out: taxes are capped at 9% for four years, and the new council lets any grant on offer go. Nothing else changes.
