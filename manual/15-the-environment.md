# The environment
> Pollution, grime, smog, noise, heat and carbon.

## Pollution

Works, mines, power stations, freight yards, ports, airports and traffic all pollute, spreading a few tiles around them. Bigger works pollute more. Cars put out the most fumes from the 1920s to the 1970s and less after, and slow traffic and long waits at crossings put out more.

Pollution lowers land value and health. Parks, woods and street trees soak up some of it, and a belt of park or woods between a works and the homes keeps most of it away. The **Pollution limit on works** district policy and scrubbers on power stations cut it at the source.

Where pollution sits for long, grime builds up in the ground and is slow to go. Grime makes people unwell and spoils wells.

## Smog

Smog is the town's pollution hanging in still air, thicker in the cold and in fog, and carried in from neighbouring towns. It's bad for health and puts people off their homes. The budget's **Garbage** section shows it, and the news says when it's bad.

## Noise

Busy roads, highways, track, freight yards and airports are noisy, and land near them is worth less. Airports are the loudest, off the ends of the runway most of all.

## Heat

Roofs and paving hold the heat, and water, trees and parks cool things down. In summer a hot neighbourhood is less pleasant to live in, and in a heat wave the elderly in hot homes are at risk. Parks cool more from 1970. Cool roofs and green roofs, as district policies, cool a district too. The **Heat** view shows where it's hot.

## Carbon

Power stations, traffic, works and heating all put out carbon. The world warms from 1980, and a town that puts out a lot warms it a little more. A warmer climate brings more hot spells, heavier rain and more gales. The People window's **Climate** section shows how warm it's got and where the town's carbon comes from, and the Future era asks for it to come down.
