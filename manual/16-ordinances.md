# Ordinances
> Town-wide laws: what each does, what it costs, and the years history gives it.

**Ordinances** in the budget, or the U key, opens the town's laws. Each is a switch: on puts it in force across the whole town, off repeals it. District policies are for one part of town; ordinances are for all of it.

Each law costs something every month for every thousand people, shown beside it at the town's size now, and some bring money in. A law can only be passed once its year has come and the town is in that year's era. Laws whose years haven't come say when they will, and ones waiting on the town's era name it. A town hall cuts what they all cost (see Services). History ends some, and a law that's had its day is repealed by itself, with a word in the news. A new law the town can pass is in the news too.

## Safety

- **Building code** (1900): fewer fires start, and they spread less.
- **Youth curfew** (1900 to 1970): less theft, and quieter shops at night.

## Health

- **Public health act** (1900): milk and water inspected, so people are healthier and fewer children die.
- **Tenement act** (1910): crowded tenements are no longer unhealthy.
- **Social housing** (1945): the poor aren't priced out of dear neighbourhoods.
- **School meals** (1940): children learn faster and are healthier.
- **Fluoridation** (1950): people on mains water are healthier.
- **Heat plan** (2000): fewer of the old and frail die in a heat wave.
- **Smoking ban** (2005): people are healthier, and trade dips a little.

## Drink and nightlife

- **Liquor licences** (1900): a little less vice, and fees from the shops. They bring nothing while prohibition is in force.
- **Sunday closing** (1900 to 1985): less vice, and less trade.
- **Prohibition** (1920 to 1933): much less vice, but the rackets grow fat on it. History repeals it in 1933.
- **Late licences** (1990): more custom and culture at night, more vice, and fees.

## Environment

- **Smoke abatement** (1905): works let out less smoke, and like the town a little less.
- **Noise bylaw** (1960): noise from traffic and planes matters less, and shops are a little less wanted.
- **Clean air act** (1970): power stations let out a quarter less smoke, but fuel costs more.
- **Tree protection** (1975): woods soak up more pollution.

## Traffic, waste, energy and culture

- **Speed limits** (1920): quieter streets, and fines.
- **Parking meters** (1935): a few less drive, and the meters pay.
- **Congestion charge** (2005): fewer cars, a little less trade, and money in.
- **Bottle deposit** (1970): less garbage.
- **Curbside recycling** (1990): half as much again recycled, where there's a recycling centre to take it.
- **Daylight saving** (1918): a lower evening peak for power.
- **Energy code** (1980): less power used.
- **Carbon price** (2020): fossil fuel costs more, and money comes in for every tonne of carbon.
- **Dog licences** (1950): people enjoy the parks more, and a little money comes in.
- **Percent for art** (1970): more culture, and more visitors.
