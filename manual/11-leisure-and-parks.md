# Leisure and parks
> What people want to do near home, and the parks, gardens and green space that give it.

## Leisure

People want somewhere to go near home: parks and gardens, sport, and culture. Each home has a leisure score, from 0 to 100, from what's within reach of it. What counts most changes with the times. Green space matters most at the start and the end of the century, sport and culture in the middle. Plenty of the kind most wanted is enough on its own, and the others add to it.

People also expect more as the years go by: a little in 1900, a fair amount by mid-century and more again later. A home with more than people expect draws them, and more so on medium and high density land, where homes have no yards of their own. A home with less than expected is a little less wanted. Green space and sport near home also make people healthier, and sport for the young cuts theft.

**Leisure near home** on a home's inspect card and in the Demand window shows the score against what's expected. The **Leisure** map view shows it across the town. When homes are held back for want of things to do, the advice says so.

## The Leisure button

**Leisure** on the toolbar has the parks and green space, with sport and culture still to come. Some green space is laid a tile at a time by dragging, like a park; the rest goes down whole, where the finger ends up.

## Parks and green space

- **Park** is laid a tile at a time: lawn, paths and trees, with a fountain, bandstand or flower beds.
- **Street trees** are planted along a road.
- **Playground** gives children somewhere to play, and a little sport.
- **Town square** is paved, with a statue and trees, and gives some culture. It becomes a **Plaza** from 1960, with a fountain pool.
- **Formal garden** has hedges, flower beds and a fountain, and draws visitors.
- **City park** is big: a lake, paths, a bandstand and many trees. It reaches far, soaks up a lot of pollution and draws visitors.
- **Allotments** (1915) are plots for growing vegetables. They become **Community gardens** from 1975.
- **Pocket park** (1965) fits a small green into a gap.
- **Woodland** is planted a tile at a time. It soaks up the most pollution, cools and holds the rain, and comes into its own over fifteen years.
- **Botanical garden** (1910) has a palm house and beds from all over, and draws many visitors.
- **Wetland reserve** (1985) holds the rain, soaks up pollution and draws birdwatchers.
- **Greenway** (1985) is a path for walking and cycling through grass and trees, laid a tile at a time.
- **Dog park** (1990) is a fenced run for dogs.

All green space raises land value nearby, the biggest parks more, soaks up pollution, cools the town in summer and holds some of the rain. A belt of it between works and homes keeps much of the smoke away. Green space that's let go for want of parks funding does less.
