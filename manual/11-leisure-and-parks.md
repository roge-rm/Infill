# Leisure and parks
> What people want to do near home, and the parks, sport and culture that give it.

## Leisure

People want somewhere to go near home: parks and gardens, sport, and culture. Each home has a leisure score, from 0 to 100, from what's within reach of it. What counts most changes with the times. Green space matters most at the start and the end of the century, sport and culture in the middle. Plenty of the kind most wanted is enough on its own, and the others add to it.

People also expect more as the years go by: a little in 1900, a fair amount by mid-century and more again later. A home with more than people expect draws them, and more so on medium and high density land, where homes have no yards of their own. A home with less than expected is a little less wanted. Green space and sport near home also make people healthier, and sport for the young cuts theft.

**Leisure near home** on a home's inspect card and in the Demand window shows the score against what's expected. The **Leisure** map view shows it across the town. When homes are held back for want of things to do, the advice says so.

## The Leisure button

**Leisure** on the toolbar has three tabs: parks and green space, sport, and culture. Some green space is laid a tile at a time by dragging, like a park. Everything else goes down whole, where the finger ends up.

Sport and culture do as much as the **Parks and leisure** funding and their staff allow, and they wear like other buildings. Some newer kinds take an older one's place over the years, as services do, and **Bring up to date** turns an old one into the new.

## Parks and green space

- **Park** is laid a tile at a time: lawn, paths and trees, with a fountain, bandstand or flower beds.
- **Street trees** are planted along a road.
- **Playground** gives children somewhere to play, and a little sport.
- **Town square** is paved, with a statue and trees, and gives some culture. It becomes a **Plaza** from 1960, with a fountain pool.
- **Formal garden** has hedges, flower beds and a fountain, and draws visitors.
- **City park** is big: a lake, paths, a bandstand and many trees. It reaches far, soaks up a lot of pollution and draws visitors.
- **Allotments** (1915) are plots for growing vegetables. They become **Community gardens** from 1975.
- **Pocket park** (1965) fits a small green into a gap.
- **Woodland** is planted a tile at a time. It soaks up the most pollution, cools and holds the rain, and comes into its own over fifteen years.
- **Botanical garden** (1910) has a palm house and beds from all over, and draws many visitors.
- **Wetland reserve** (1985) holds the rain, soaks up pollution and draws birdwatchers.
- **Greenway** (1985) is a path for walking and cycling through grass and trees, laid a tile at a time.
- **Dog park** (1990) is a fenced run for dogs.

All green space raises land value nearby, the biggest parks more, soaks up pollution, cools the town in summer and holds some of the rain. A belt of it between works and homes keeps much of the smoke away. Green space that's let go for want of funding does less.

## Sport

- **Sports ground** has a pitch, a little stand and a clubhouse. It becomes **Lit playing fields** from 1980.
- **Public baths** have a pool and slipper baths. They become a **Swimming pool** from 1935, and an **Aquatic centre** from 1985, which draws visitors too.
- **Tennis courts** (1910) are small and cheap.
- **Ice rink** (1920) gives sport and a little culture.
- **Ballpark** (1910) draws crowds from out of town.
- **Golf course** (1910) is big and green, and soaks up pollution like a park.
- **Recreation centre** (1950) has a gym, rooms and a court.
- **Stadium** (1960) draws the biggest crowds.
- **Arena** (1965) has sport and concerts under one roof.
- **Skate park** (1995) gives the young somewhere to go.

Sport near home cuts theft among the young.

## Culture

- **Bandstand** is a summer evening's music in the park. It goes out of fashion from 1930.
- **Variety theatre** is music hall and acts. It gives way to the **Picture palace** in 1925, whose heyday runs to the 1950s, and to the **Multiplex** in 1985.
- **Opera house**, **Museum** and, from 1920, **Art gallery** draw visitors, and offices like to be near them.
- **Concert hall** (1960) has a big folded roof over a glass foyer.
- **Zoo** (1910) is green as well as a draw.
- **Fairground** has a big wheel and a carousel. It gives way to the **Amusement park** in 1955.
- **Drive-in** (1950) has a big screen and rows of parking. It goes out of fashion from 1975.
- **Aquarium** (1990) and **Convention centre** (1975) draw many visitors.

Culture nearby also brings custom to the shops round it.
